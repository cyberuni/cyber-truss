A truss is a rigid net of members in which load applied at *any* point redistributes
through the whole structure. That is the mental image: change entering anywhere, and the
whole repository settling around it.

*Lattice* is the concept the system is about — used in prose and specs, never as the
wordmark. Three readings carry weight, and each one leaves a mark on the design.

## Crystal lattice

The structure shape-memory alloys return to. Bend one and it goes back — not because
anything searched for the original shape, but because that shape is where the material
is at rest.

Levinthal's paradox tells the same story about proteins: one cannot search all its
conformations, yet it folds in milliseconds, because the energy landscape is
funnel-shaped. The system does not *search* for the settled state either; the shape of
the landscape makes reaching it the path of least resistance. **Settling should be
downhill; staying strained should be uphill.**

## A graph of interconnected nodes

Drag one, release, and the whole thing settles.

<LatticeGraph />

The nodes are a repository's [artifact-sets](/cyber-truss/model/artifact-sets/) — the PRD,
the mockups, the acceptance criteria, the spec, the ADRs, the docs, the code, the repo
config, the test plan, the tests, CI, the deploy config, the release. An edge is a
[connection](/cyber-truss/model/connections/): the fact that changing one of the two
forces a change in the other. It is a relation, not an arrow, so the load travels either
way along it.

They are placed the way a lifecycle diagram places them, intent on the left and the
running system on the right, because that is the arrangement a reader already knows. The
placement is a convenience of the drawing and nothing more — no artifact-set is upstream
of another, and the strain crosses a member in whichever direction the change arrived
from. What the system holds is the edge set; where a node sits on the page it does not.

Adding a node forms new edges and changes the spacing: same ripple, larger structure.
Which properties have to converge and which are free is the subject of
[Confluence](/cyber-truss/model/confluence/).

## Order-theoretic lattice

A lattice has a *join*: a unique least upper bound for any two elements. Merge into a
join-semilattice is therefore confluent by construction — no two routes can reach
different answers, because the answer is *defined* as the least thing above both.

This is the reading the guarantee rests on, and buying it is what the rest of the model
is for.
