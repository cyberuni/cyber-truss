Novelists split into plotters, who outline before they draft, and discovery writers, who
find the story by writing it. It is the same split as spec-first and code-first. The craft
advice is the same too: a twist found in the draft has to be planted back through the
chapters before it, or it reads as unearned. This example runs that planting through the
model.

## The system

A fantasy novel of twenty chapters, drafted as far as chapter 14.

| Artifact-set | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `{premise}` | The synopsis: what the book is about and how it ends |
| `{outline}` | Chapter-by-chapter beats: who does what, and when the reader learns it |
| `{story bible}` | Characters, backstories, world rules, and the timeline |
| `{manuscript}` | The chapter drafts |

The chapters are one artifact, not twenty. A continuity rule is written once for the
manuscript, not once per chapter, for the reason given under
[Artifact](/cyber-truss/model/artifact-sets/#artifact).

Connections:

- `{premise}` to `{outline}`
- `{outline}` to `{manuscript}`
- `{story bible}` to `{outline}`
- `{story bible}` to `{manuscript}`

The last connection holds what the outline does not state: eye colour, how far a horse
travels in a day, where a character was in a given year.

The premise reads: *Ilse, heir to a fallen house, learns who destroyed her family and must
decide whether revenge is worth becoming what she hates.* The outline names the betrayer
as Chancellor Aubrec, a minor court official, revealed in chapter 16. The story bible says
Ilse's mentor, Maren, spent the year the house fell at sea.

## Workflows

| Workflow | Input | Owned | Output | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plotting | `{premise}` | `{outline}` | none | one link |
| Drafting | `{outline}` | `{manuscript}` | none | one link |
| Reverse outlining | `{manuscript}` | `{outline}` | none | one link |
| Worldbuilding | `{story bible}` | `{outline}` | none | one link |
| Continuity pass | `{story bible}` | `{manuscript}` | none | one link |
| Canon update | `{manuscript}` | `{story bible}` | none | one link |

Every set is revised in place until the book is published, so each is
[owned](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#three-roles) by the workflows that write it and none is
an output. A printed edition would be an output: it cannot be revised, only superseded by
the next edition.

Two edges carry a pair of workflows with opposite
[roles](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#direction-lives-in-the-workflow). On
`{story bible}` to `{manuscript}`, continuity pass treats the bible as canon: the draft
must agree with it. Canon update treats the draft as the record: what a chapter
establishes is written back into the bible. On `{outline}` to `{manuscript}`, drafting
writes chapters from the outline, and reverse outlining, a technique discovery writers use,
rebuilds the outline from the chapters. A book written only by plotting needs the first of
each pair. A book where discoveries happen in the draft needs both, and this author works
both ways.

## The change

While drafting chapter 14, the author sees that the betrayal lands harder if it comes from
Maren. They write the scene in which Ilse finds Maren's seal on the letter that doomed her
family. The diff touches chapter 14 only.

## Variant A: the book has an outline

### Expected run

1. **Lift.** The change touches `{manuscript}`.
2. **Find candidates.** Drafting and continuity pass own `{manuscript}`, so both are
   [upstream candidates](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#a-change-finds-its-candidates).
   Reverse outlining and canon update read the manuscript, so both are downstream
   candidates.
3. **Distill.** Distillation
   [reads back](/cyber-truss/model/canonical-execution/#distillation-reads-backward) along
   drafting to `{outline}`, which names Aubrec, and along continuity pass to
   `{story bible}`, which puts Maren at sea. Both contradict the chapter, so the walk stops
   at each. Intent: *the person who betrayed Ilse's family is the person she trusts most,
   Maren.* The name stays in the intent. A reading that kept only *someone Ilse trusts*
   would let a replay choose a different character, and what the author found was who, not
   a category.
4. **Derive criteria.**
   - Maren betrayed the house, and Aubrec did not.
   - The reveal comes in chapter 14.
   - At least two earlier scenes admit both readings of Maren: innocent on a first
     reading, damning on a second.
   - Maren's backstory places her where she could have sealed the letter.
   - The climax still turns on Ilse's choice about revenge.

   The first and fourth criteria
   [replace](/cyber-truss/model/join/#replacing-a-criterion-is-not-a-join) what the outline
   and the bible state. They are not joined with it.
5. **Select**, by the [six steps](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#how-workflows-are-selected).
   - *Check strain.* Drafting and continuity pass are strained, because the sets the walk
     read contradict the criteria. Reverse outlining and canon update are strained, because
     the outline and the bible no longer meet what the changed chapter states.
   - The walk found `{outline}` and `{story bible}` strained. Three workflows own the
     outline: plotting, reverse outlining, and worldbuilding. Canon update alone owns the
     bible.
   - *Break ties.* Of the three that own the outline, the intent keeps chapter 14, so the
     outline must follow the manuscript. Reverse outlining reads the manuscript. Plotting
     reads a premise that names no betrayer, and worldbuilding reads a bible that is itself
     stale. Reverse outlining is chosen.
   - *Wait on inputs.* Drafting waits for the outline, and continuity pass waits for the
     bible. Reverse outlining and canon update read the manuscript, which is also strained,
     and the workflows that write the manuscript are waiting on them. Read literally, the
     rule deadlocks here. The run proceeds by treating the manuscript as settled, because
     the intent says chapter 14 stands, so reverse outlining and canon update start.
6. **Replay.** Reverse outlining rebuilds the outline to name Maren, plant her in chapters
   3 and 9, and end on Ilse's choice about her. Canon update puts Maren ashore in the year
   the house fell. With both inputs settled, drafting derives setup scenes for chapters 3
   and 9 and a new ending in chapters 18 to 20, which had Ilse executing Aubrec and now has
   her facing someone she loves. Continuity pass derives the chapters' facts against the
   run's criteria. Neither reads the author's chapter 14, because it landed in a set they
   own.

   The amended bible is a change of its own, and worldbuilding reads it. Chapter 2 of the
   old outline had Maren's ship coming into port. Reverse outlining rebuilt the outline
   against the same criteria, so if it rebuilt chapter 2 the strain is already gone. If
   not, worldbuilding amends chapter 2 the way reverse outlining would have, in one extra
   cycle, and the two writes [join](/cyber-truss/model/join/) at the outline's
   [controller](/cyber-truss/model/controller/#the-join-happens-at-the-controller).
   `{premise}` to `{outline}` holds, because the premise names no betrayer and the reworked
   climax still turns on revenge.
7. **Compare.** The author's chapter 14 meets the reveal criterion. The setup criterion
   has holes: nothing before chapter 14 supports the reveal, which is why the scene read as
   a twist rather than a turn. Drafting derived its own chapter 14, and both versions meet
   the criteria. Prose is a
   [coordinate](/cyber-truss/model/confluence/#what-confluence-is-claimed-over), so the
   choice between the two chapters is a choice between settled states. The author keeps
   their own scene, and the choice is recorded. If drafting and reverse outlining place the
   setup in different chapters, both placements meet *at least two earlier scenes*, and
   that choice is recorded too.

### Settled state

- `{premise}` is unchanged.
- `{outline}` names Maren, plants her in chapters 3 and 9, and ends on Ilse's choice about
  her.
- `{story bible}` puts Maren ashore in the year the house fell.
- `{manuscript}` carries the author's chapter 14, with derived setup scenes and a derived
  ending.

### Status: Unresolved

The run carries the twist back through the outline and out to the chapters that need it,
once the wait in step 5 is broken. The rule as written makes a workflow wait on a strained
input, and on an edge with opposite roles each side waits on the other. The run above
breaks the cycle by treating the set the intent keeps as settled. The model does not say
that, and until it does this run can stall.

The reverse workflows matter. With only plotting, drafting, worldbuilding, and continuity
pass, nothing owns `{outline}` or `{story bible}` from the manuscript's side. Step 6 of
selection then
[raises the strain as an obligation](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#how-workflows-are-selected)
against both sets, and the gap in the declarations is visible: the author needs reverse
outlining and canon update, and has not declared them. The run no longer ends silently with
the outline still naming Aubrec.

The [leash](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#leash) does not stop drafting from rewriting three
chapters of the ending. They are owned writes and they meet the criteria. An author would
want to approve that, and would have to tighten the leash on `{manuscript}` to get it.

The run also leaves one choice to whichever state it reaches. The criteria say Aubrec did
not betray the house, and nothing else about him. One replay keeps him as a red herring and
another cuts him, and both states meet the criteria.
[Confluence](/cyber-truss/model/confluence/#what-confluence-is-claimed-over) lists actors
as the usual topology, but topology is only what the criteria constrain, so a character no
criterion names is a coordinate. An author would not call a character a coordinate. The
model's reply is that a difference that matters is a criterion nobody stated, and stating
it starts the next iteration. The reply is consistent, and the choice is recorded rather
than left to timing.

## Variant B: the author writes without an outline

The book has no `{outline}`. The connections are `{premise}` to `{manuscript}` and
`{story bible}` to `{manuscript}`. Drafting reads `{premise}` and owns `{manuscript}`.
Continuity pass and canon update are unchanged.

### Expected run

1. **Lift.** The change touches `{manuscript}`.
2. **Find candidates.** Drafting and continuity pass own `{manuscript}`, so they are
   upstream candidates. Canon update reads it, so it is a downstream candidate.
3. **Distill.** Along drafting, distillation reads `{premise}`, the workflow's declared
   start, which names no betrayer, so the walk stops there. Along continuity pass it reads
   `{story bible}`, which puts Maren at sea. The intent is the same as variant A.
4. **Derive criteria.** The same criteria as variant A.
5. **Select.**
   - *Check strain.* The premise states none of the run's criteria, so drafting has
     [missing strain](/cyber-truss/model/connections/#missing). The bible contradicts the
     criteria, so continuity pass is strained, and canon update is strained because the
     bible no longer meets the changed chapter. Canon update alone owns the bible.
   - *Wait on inputs.* Continuity pass waits for the bible. Canon update reads the
     manuscript, which continuity pass writes, and the same deadlock as variant A follows
     unless the manuscript is treated as settled. Drafting reads the premise, which nothing
     changes, so it starts.
6. **Replay.** Drafting starts at `{premise}`, which is too coarse to hold a setup beat, so
   the Request passes through it. Drafting derives setup scenes in chapters 3 and 9 and a
   new ending. Canon update puts Maren ashore. Continuity pass then derives the chapters'
   facts.
7. **Compare.** The same holes as variant A. Nothing before chapter 14 supported the reveal,
   and drafting's scenes fill them.

### Settled state

- `{premise}` is unchanged.
- `{story bible}` puts Maren ashore.
- `{manuscript}` carries the author's chapter 14, with setup in chapters 3 and 9 and a
  derived ending.

### Status: Unresolved

The run finishes the book. The setup criterion survives, because the change landed in a set
drafting owns, and drafting's vocabulary holds scenes where continuity pass's vocabulary of
facts could not. The hole the comparison reports reaches the manuscript through drafting.

Three things remain open.

- **Waiting on an input the intent keeps.** The same deadlock as variant A, between
  continuity pass and canon update.
- **Missing strain against an input too coarse to hold it.** The premise is the only input
  drafting has, and a premise has no place for a setup beat. In the pagination example a PRD
  passed the rule through to a spec below it. Here nothing lies between the premise and the
  chapters, so the criterion's only home is the manuscript itself. The model does not say
  whether the strain on `{premise}` to `{manuscript}` clears once the replay judges the
  premise too coarse, or stays open. That depends on
  [how levels are identified](/cyber-truss/model/specification/#specifications-exist-at-every-level),
  which is still open.
- **Coupling inside one artifact-set.** Once the run closes, the setup in chapter 3 is held
  by nothing but the chapter. A later edit that removes it strains no connection. In
  variant A the outline carries the beat, and cutting it strains `{outline}` to
  `{manuscript}`. Inside the set, keeping chapter 3 consistent with chapter 14 is
  [the controller's job](/cyber-truss/model/controller/#between-sets-within-a-set), and
  nothing records the beat for the controller to keep.

## What it tests

- [Direction in the workflow](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#direction-lives-in-the-workflow).
  Fiction uses both directions on the same edges: canon constrains the draft, and the draft
  amends canon. Without the reverse workflows, selection raises the strain as an obligation
  and names the missing declarations.
- [How workflows are selected](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#how-workflows-are-selected).
  Three workflows own the outline, and the intent breaks the tie toward reverse outlining.
  Waiting on inputs deadlocks on an edge with opposite roles unless the set the intent keeps
  counts as settled.
- [Candidates](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#a-change-finds-its-candidates) choosing the
  workflow with the right vocabulary. In variant B the setup criterion survives because the
  change landed in a set drafting owns.
- [Missing strain](/cyber-truss/model/connections/#missing) where the only input is too
  coarse to state the missing criterion, and no finer set exists.
- [Intent and criteria](/cyber-truss/model/specification/#a-specification-is-intent-plus-criteria)
  in a field where quality is argued. Whether a twist is earned is a craft judgement, but
  *two earlier scenes admit both readings* can be checked by a reader who has not reached
  chapter 14.
- [Distillation](/cyber-truss/model/canonical-execution/#distillation-reads-backward)
  keeping a proper name. An intent abstract enough to drop the name lets a replay swap the
  author's discovery for a different one that meets the same criteria.
- [The inversion](/cyber-truss/model/canonical-execution/#the-inversion) in creative work.
  The author's scene is treated as a prediction, and it survives the comparison only
  because prose is a coordinate. If a style guide constrained the prose, the prose would be
  topology, and the comparison could reject the author's own sentences.
- The [leash](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#leash) default on owned writes. It lets an agent
  rewrite an ending that meets the criteria, which an author may not accept.
- Change reaching backwards in reading order. The setup lands in chapters the author
  thought were finished. Connections are
  [undirected](/cyber-truss/model/connections/#connections-are-undirected), and the reach
  they give is not bound to the order a reader meets the chapters in.
- Coupling inside one artifact-set. The model sees connections between sets. The
  consistency fiction depends on most, one chapter against another, lies within a set, and
  variant B shows what is lost when no connection stands in for it.
- Actors as topology. Variant A leaves a character's survival to whichever settled state
  the run reaches.
