A campaign is planned top-down: a brief, then messaging, then ads and landing pages. It
changes bottom-up, because the ads are where results arrive. This example takes a
headline that wins in market and asks how far up the campaign its result should reach.

## The system

A paid campaign for Tally, a budgeting app, aimed at people aged 25 to 34.

| Artifact-set | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `{brief}` | Objective, audience, the single-minded proposition, budget, and target cost per sign-up |
| `{messaging}` | The messaging framework: headline themes, proof points, and the substantiation on file for each claim |
| `{ads}` | Ad copy and visuals for paid social and search, with their variants |
| `{launches}` | Which ad variants are live, from when, and on what budget |
| `{landing page}` | The page the ads send traffic to |
| `{campaign results}` | Spend, clicks, sign-ups, and cost per sign-up for each ad and page variant |

Ad copy and a launch are different kinds of write. Copy can be revised in place until it
runs. A launch spends money the moment it serves, and the spend cannot be recalled. Pausing
it is a new launch decision, not a revision of the old one. So `{ads}` is
[owned](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#three-roles) by the workflow that writes it, and
`{launches}` is its output.

`{campaign results}` is written by the ad platform's and the analytics tool's reporting,
its [controller](/cyber-truss/model/controller/). No workflow writes it.

Connections:

- `{brief}` to `{messaging}`
- `{messaging}` to `{ads}`
- `{messaging}` to `{landing page}`
- `{ads}` to `{landing page}`
- `{ads}` to `{launches}`: every live launch runs an ad as it is written
- `{launches}` to `{campaign results}`: every live launch meets the brief's target cost per
  sign-up
- `{landing page}` to `{campaign results}`: every live page variant meets the same target

The fourth connection is message match: a visitor who clicks on a promise expects to find it
at the top of the page. The framework does not say which headline a page must open on for a
given ad, so the match cannot be checked through `{messaging}`. It is the same kind of
sibling connection as the API docs in the
[component library example](/cyber-truss/examples/component-library-bug-fix/). Which end
yields is declared by the workflow below, not by the connection.

The brief's proposition is *Tally shows you where your money goes.* The framework allows
headlines about clarity and control. It lists savings as a proof point, *active users save
a median of $612 in their first year*, backed by a 2025 analysis of 40,000 accounts. The
figure may appear in body copy, with its basis stated.

## Workflows

| Workflow | Input | Owned | Output | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Campaign planning | `{brief}` | `{messaging}` | none | one link |
| Creative production | `{messaging}` | `{ads}` | `{launches}` | chain |
| Page build | `{messaging}` | `{landing page}` | none | one link |
| Message match | `{ads}` | `{landing page}` | none | one link |

The declarations are top-down, the way the team plans. Every workflow
[reads the set above it and writes the set below](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#direction-lives-in-the-workflow),
and message match makes the ads the specification of the page. Only campaign planning owns
`{messaging}`, and it reads the brief. No workflow owns `{brief}`.

Creative production keeps the default [leash](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#leash). Its
edits to ad copy proceed when the criteria pass. A launch commits spend, so it needs the
performance marketer's approval.

## The change

Three weeks in, the team learns that the savings figure sells the app better than clarity
does. The finding reaches the campaign by one of two routes, one per variant.

## Variant A: the change lands in an ad

A performance marketer writes a new paid social headline in the ad platform, *Tally users
save $612 in their first year*, and launches it. Within a week it has three times the
click-through rate of the control headline and meets the target cost per sign-up. The change
touches `{ads}` and `{launches}`.

### Expected run

1. **Lift.** The change touches `{ads}` and `{launches}`.
2. **Find candidates.** Creative production owns `{ads}` and outputs `{launches}`, so it is
   an [upstream candidate](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#a-change-finds-its-candidates).
   Message match reads `{ads}` as an input, so it is a downstream candidate.
3. **Distill.** The walk back along creative production reaches `{messaging}`, which keeps
   savings in body copy. That contradicts what the headline implies, so the walk stops
   there. Intent: *lead paid social with the savings figure.* The results are why the
   marketer kept the headline. They are not what the headline is for.
4. **Criteria.**
   - Paid social headlines, and the page they send traffic to, may lead with the savings
     figure.
   - Every figure states its basis where it appears.
   - A visitor from a savings ad finds the figure above the fold.
5. **Select.**
   - Creative production is strained, because `{messaging}` contradicts the first
     criterion.
   - `{messaging}` is a set the walk read and found contradicting, so campaign planning,
     which owns it, is selected.
   - Message match is strained. The page it owns opens on clarity and never states the
     figure, so it no longer meets what the changed ads promise.
   - The live launch is strained. It runs a figure with no basis, and a launch is an
     output, so the strain clears only by a superseding launch. Until then it is held as
     [obligation](/cyber-truss/model/connections/#obligation).
   - Creative production waits, because its input `{messaging}` is strained. Message match
     waits, because its input `{ads}` is strained until creative production runs.
6. **Replay.** Campaign planning starts at `{brief}`, its input. At `{messaging}` two
   criteria reach the join: the brief's *one proposition in every channel*, carried by the
   framework as *savings stays in body copy*, and the run's *paid social may lead with
   savings*. The intent is about paid social and says nothing about the other channels, so
   it does not [replace](/cyber-truss/model/join/#replacing-a-criterion-is-not-a-join) the
   proposition. The two stand together and contradict each other. That is a
   [conflict](/cyber-truss/model/join/#conflict), and the run stops.
7. **Decide.** The criterion the run would override comes from the brief, and no workflow
   owns `{brief}`, so the conflict goes to the brand lead. The brand lead decides that paid
   social may lead with savings until the campaign ends, as a test, and that the
   proposition holds everywhere else. The decision is recorded, lands in `{messaging}` and
   `{brief}`, the sets it affects, and creates the next version of the criteria.
8. **Replay, continued.** Campaign planning amends the framework under the new version.
   `{messaging}` is no longer strained, so creative production runs. It does not read the
   arriving headline, because the headline landed in a set it owns. From the amended
   framework it derives savings-led paid social ads that state the basis. Then message
   match derives a landing page variant for paid social traffic that opens on the figure.
9. **Compare.**
   - The arriving headline has a hole: it states the figure with no basis.
   - The brief's target cost per sign-up is a criterion over the outcome. The arriving
     launch has results in `{campaign results}` and meets it. The replayed ads have never
     run.
   - The team keeps the marketer's headline, the only version with results, and adds a
     basis line. The replayed ads also meet the criteria, so the choice between them is
     recorded.
10. **Leash.** The amended framework, the ad copy, and the page variant are owned writes,
    and the criteria pass, so they proceed. Relaunching the headline with its basis line is
    an output, so it goes to the performance marketer for approval. Once live, it
    supersedes the arriving launch and the obligation clears.

### Settled state

- `{brief}` keeps its proposition, with a recorded exception for paid social.
- `{messaging}` permits savings-led headlines in paid social and on its landing page
  variant, with the basis stated.
- `{ads}` holds the marketer's headline with a basis line.
- `{launches}` records the original launch, superseded by the relaunch with the basis line.
- `{landing page}` has a paid social variant that opens on the figure.
- `{campaign results}` is unchanged by the run. Had the team launched a replayed ad
  instead, its cost per sign-up would be held as obligation strain on `{launches}` to
  `{campaign results}` until the reporting caught up.

### Status: Holds

The run carries the result from one ad up to the brief, where positioning is decided, and
stops there for a person. That is the behaviour the team would want. An ad test informs the
positioning and does not rewrite it.

The route up is now a workflow, not a side effect. The walk finds `{messaging}`
contradicting the change, selection routes that strain to campaign planning, and its replay
from the brief is where the conflict surfaces. The declared roles make that the only route.
No workflow owns `{messaging}` except one that reads the brief, and no workflow owns
`{brief}` at all, so a result reaches positioning only through a recorded decision.

The roles also separate two things the earlier run treated as one. Rewriting copy is
revisable and proceeds on the criteria. Relaunching spends money and waits for a person.

## Variant B: the change lands on the landing page

Instead, a web editor who has seen the same analysis changes the landing page headline to
*Save $612 in your first year*, to lift conversion. The ads are unchanged. The change
touches `{landing page}`.

### Expected run

1. **Lift.** The change touches `{landing page}`.
2. **Find candidates.** Page build and message match both own `{landing page}`, so both are
   upstream candidates. No workflow reads `{landing page}` as an input, so there are no
   downstream candidates.
3. **Distill.** The walk back along page build reaches `{messaging}`, which contradicts the
   new headline, and stops. The walk along message match reaches `{ads}`, which hold the
   paid social and search ads that send traffic to the page, all promising clarity, and
   stops there. Email sends traffic too. A change to the page names no channel, and the
   walks confirm the page serves all of them. Intent: *lead with the savings figure wherever
   visitors land.*
4. **Criteria.**
   - The landing page, and every ad that sends traffic to it, may lead with the savings
     figure.
   - Every figure states its basis where it appears.
   - Every ad makes a promise the page keeps.
5. **Select.**
   - Page build is strained, because `{messaging}` contradicts the first criterion.
   - Message match is strained, because `{ads}` contradicts it.
   - Both contradicting sets were read by the walks, so campaign planning, which owns
     `{messaging}`, and creative production, which owns `{ads}`, are selected too.
   - No output changed, so nothing is held as obligation yet.
   - Page build and creative production wait on `{messaging}`. Message match waits on
     `{ads}`.
6. **Replay.** Campaign planning starts at `{brief}`. At `{messaging}` the run's criterion
   meets the brief's proposition, and this time the run's criterion covers every channel,
   which is the proposition itself. The two contradict each other, and the run stops.
7. **Decide.** No workflow owns `{brief}`, so no run can clear the strain on it. It is raised
   as an obligation against the brief, as
   [selection step 6](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#how-workflows-are-selected) requires,
   and the conflict goes to the brand lead. The brand lead either replaces the proposition
   with savings or refuses the change.
8. **Compare.** Whatever the decision, the editor's headline has the same hole as variant
   A's: it gives no basis. The page variant has no results yet, so its cost per sign-up is
   held as obligation strain on `{landing page}` to `{campaign results}`.

### Settled state

Either savings leads in every channel under a new proposition, or the change is refused.
Neither is variant A's state.

### Status: Unresolved

Variants A and B carry one finding, that savings sells better than clarity to this
audience, and they settle in states that meet different criteria.
[Confluence](/cyber-truss/model/confluence/#the-claim) is claimed over criteria, so this is
not a harmless difference between settled states. If the two changes carry one intent,
confluence is lost.

The candidates and the walks do not fix it. They make it exact. From an ad, the only
upstream candidate is creative production, and its walk reads one channel's framework. From
the page, the upstream candidates are page build and message match, and message match's walk
reads every channel's ads. There are no downstream candidates to widen or narrow either
change. Distillation reads what its walks reach, so each keeps the scope of the artifact the
change landed in, now with the model's own structure behind it rather than the distiller's
taste.

The finding came from paid social results, and those live in `{campaign results}`. No
workflow reads that set, so neither walk reaches the evidence. Selection step 6 does not
catch this. It raises strain that no declared workflow can clear, and `{campaign results}`
is not strained. It is only unread. The model has no step for evidence that no workflow
reads.

Declaring a workflow that reads `{campaign results}` and owns `{messaging}` would not put the
evidence on the walks either. Each walk stops at the first set that contradicts the change,
and in both variants that set is `{messaging}` or `{ads}`, before the walk could reach the
results. Whether distillation should read further, and whether it would then narrow
variant B to paid social, is the question
[Can distillation be made stable?](/cyber-truss/model/open-questions/#can-distillation-be-made-stable)
leaves open. This pair gives it a case where the instability comes from the structure of the
field, not from the distiller.

The decision in step 7 can hide the divergence. A brand lead facing variant B's wider
conflict can choose variant A's narrower exception, and then the two runs agree. They agree
because a person chose, and a run with no person in it would not.

## What it tests

- [Outputs](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#three-roles) against owned sets in one workflow.
  Ad copy is revised in place. A launch spends money, so strain on it clears only by a
  superseding launch.
- The [leash](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#leash) on spend. Copy and pages proceed on the
  criteria. Every launch waits for the performance marketer.
- [Selection](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#how-workflows-are-selected) that routes strain the
  walk found. `{messaging}` contradicts the change, so campaign planning is selected even
  though the change never touched a set it spans.
- A sibling connection outside software. Message match relates two implementations of
  `{messaging}` that the framework does not reach, as the component library's API docs
  relate to its code.
- [Conflict](/cyber-truss/model/join/#conflict) against
  [replacement](/cyber-truss/model/join/#replacing-a-criterion-is-not-a-join). Variant A's
  intent narrows the proposition without replacing it, so the two criteria stand together
  and conflict.
- Criteria that can only be evaluated in the world. Target cost per sign-up is a criterion
  over the outcome, and the outcome needs spend. [The inversion](/cyber-truss/model/canonical-execution/#the-inversion)
  treats the arriving change as a prediction and the replay as the check. For this
  criterion the arriving change is the only evidence, so a replayed launch is held as
  obligation strain until `{campaign results}` reports on it.
- Scope inherited from the entry artifact. Variant B distils a wider intent because the
  page it landed on serves more channels, and the backward walk follows that structure
  rather than correcting it.
- Evidence no workflow reads. `{campaign results}` holds the finding, and neither the walks
  nor selection step 6 reach an unread set.
- A set no workflow owns. Positioning is decided in `{brief}`, which no workflow writes, so
  both variants reach it as a conflict for a person, and variant B raises it as an
  obligation under step 6. The
  [recorded decision](/cyber-truss/model/canonical-execution/#cycles-must-come-to-rest) is
  where the strategy call is made, and it lands in the sets it affects.
- Legal review is left out. Most campaigns clear claims through a review gate. This example
  folds that review into the substantiation criterion rather than modelling it as a
  workflow.
