A trader reads a headline and buys before writing down why. Trading has the problem the
model starts from in its sharpest form. A thesis written after the trade is written by
someone who has watched the price move, and it fits the trade for that reason. The field's
usual fix is the one [preregistration](/cyber-truss/model/workflows/#preregistered-study)
uses: no order goes in without a thesis. That rule is a gate, and removing gates is what
the model is for.

## The system

A long-only equity portfolio run by one trader.

| Artifact-set | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `{mandate}` | The investment policy: position and sector limits, and what every thesis must state |
| `{theses}` | One document per holding: why it is held, and the condition that would prove it wrong |
| `{orders}` | Every order sent to the broker: ticker, side, size, and time |
| `{portfolio}` | The positions at the broker, as the fills of `{orders}` leave them |
| `{market data}` | Prices, company announcements, and analyst notes, each with a timestamp |

`{orders}` and `{portfolio}` do not live in git. They are the broker's records. The model
does not require an artifact to be a file, and the
[workflow catalog](/cyber-truss/model/workflows/#double-entry-bookkeeping) already
includes a ledger.

An order, once it fills, cannot be recalled. A later order can offset it, and nothing can
revise it. That makes `{orders}` an
[output](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#three-roles) wherever a workflow writes it.
`{portfolio}` is state: the broker's fills are its
[controller](/cyber-truss/model/controller/), and a workflow changes it only by emitting an
order.

`{market data}` is written by a feed job, its controller. No workflow writes it. It is
inside the system only because it is declared here, and once declared, a new announcement
is a change like any other.

Connections:

- `{mandate}` to `{portfolio}`: no position above 5% of the portfolio, and no sector above
  25%.
- `{mandate}` to `{theses}`: every thesis states an invalidation condition.
- `{theses}` to `{portfolio}`: every position has a thesis, and no position is held past its
  invalidation.
- `{orders}` to `{portfolio}`: every position is the net of the orders that filled.
- `{market data}` to `{theses}`: no thesis stands while market data meets its invalidation
  condition, and the data is no older than one trading day.

Before the change, semiconductors are 22% of the portfolio.

## Workflows

| Workflow | Input | Owned | Output | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thesis-first trade | `{market data}` | `{theses}`, `{portfolio}` | `{orders}` | chain |
| Rebalance | `{mandate}` | `{portfolio}` | `{orders}` | one link |
| Thesis review | `{mandate}` | `{theses}` | none | one link |

Thesis-first trade reads the market, writes a thesis, decides a position, and emits the
order that reaches it. It
[never writes its inputs](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#direction-lives-in-the-workflow), so
no replay can keep a thesis alive by amending the data it is tested against. A trade does
not change the policy, so rebalance and thesis review read `{mandate}` and never write it.

Both trading workflows keep the default
[leash](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#leash). Writes to `{theses}` and `{portfolio}` proceed
when the criteria pass. Emitting an order needs a person's approval, because an order
cannot be taken back.

## The change

Orrin Micro announces a recall of its flagship chip. Within minutes the trader buys shares
of its competitor, Kessler Semi, in the broker's app. No thesis is written. Variants A and
B differ in the size of the order. Variant C is a later announcement, and only the market
data changes.

## Variant A: the trade breaks the mandate

The order takes Kessler Semi to 8% of the portfolio and semiconductors to 30%.

### Expected run

1. **Lift.** The change touches `{orders}`, and the fill touches `{portfolio}`.
2. **Find candidates.** Thesis-first trade and rebalance both own `{portfolio}` and output
   `{orders}`, so the order bypassed both. They are
   [upstream candidates](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#a-change-finds-its-candidates). No
   workflow reads `{orders}` or `{portfolio}` as an input, so there are no downstream
   candidates.
3. **Distill.** The walk back along thesis-first trade reaches `{theses}`, which holds
   nothing for Kessler Semi, and then `{market data}` as it stood when the order went in,
   which holds Orrin's recall announcement. The walk along rebalance reaches `{mandate}`.
   Intent: *hold Kessler Semi for the market share it gains from Orrin's recall.* The size is
   expression. It records how sure the trader felt in the minute after the headline.
4. **Criteria.**
   - A thesis states the market-share mechanism and a condition that would prove it wrong:
     Orrin ships a replacement chip within one quarter.
   - The position is at most 5% of the portfolio, and semiconductors at most 25%.
5. **Select.**
   - Thesis-first trade is strained. The walk read `{theses}`, which lacks a thesis the
     criteria require. That is [missing strain](/cyber-truss/model/connections/#missing).
   - Rebalance is strained. `{mandate}` contradicts the position and the sector weight.
   - The arriving order is strained too. It is an output, so it cannot be revised. The
     strain clears only when a superseding order is emitted, and until then it is held as
     [obligation](/cyber-truss/model/connections/#obligation).
   - Neither workflow has a strained input, so neither waits.
6. **Replay.** Neither workflow reads the arriving order, because it landed in a set they
   output. Thesis-first trade starts at `{market data}`, its input, and the Request
   [passes through](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#a-replay-starts-at-the-workflows-start) to
   `{theses}`. There it writes the thesis and derives a position of at most 5%. Rebalance's
   Request passes through `{mandate}` the same way and derives the sales that bring the
   position and the sector within the limits.
7. **Compare.** The builder lens reports holes: no thesis, and two limits broken. The
   replayed position cannot replace the arriving one. The order has filled and the price
   has moved, so the state before the trade no longer exists. What the replay produces is a
   new sell order. Bringing the sector to 25% means selling 5 points of semiconductors, and
   the replay can take them all from Kessler Semi or split them with the other chip
   holdings. Both meet the criteria, and the choice is recorded.
8. **Leash.** The thesis is an owned write and the criteria pass, so it proceeds. The sell
   order is an output, so it goes to the trader for approval. Once approved and filled, it
   supersedes the arriving order, and the obligation clears.
9. **Propagate.** The new thesis states an invalidation condition, so `{mandate}` to
   `{theses}` holds. Market data does not meet that condition, so `{market data}` to
   `{theses}` holds. Nothing else is strained.

### Settled state

- `{mandate}` is unchanged.
- `{theses}` holds a Kessler Semi thesis with its invalidation condition.
- `{orders}` holds the arriving buy and the approved sell that offsets part of it.
- `{portfolio}` is within both limits.

### Status: Holds

The run reaches a state that meets the criteria, and it passes the leash: the one
irreversible write it makes waits for a person. The criteria are stated over the outcome,
and a later sale meets them as well as a smaller first order would have.

The roles now explain the breach window rather than only recording it. The arriving order
is an output, so no run can undo it. Its strain is held as obligation from the moment it
fills until a superseding order fills, and the window is that obligation's lifetime. It
includes the time the sell order waits for approval.

What the model still does not say is how long that obligation may be carried. The
[proposed boundary](/cyber-truss/model/open-questions/#what-vehicle-holds-pending-requests)
blocks carried strain at a merge, and a broker order has no merge. The workflow's
[strain policy](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#what-a-workflow-declares) is where a limit
belongs, such as *mandate strain on an order is cleared within one trading session*. This
instance does not state one, and the only earlier point a block could act is the order
ticket, which is the gate this example set out to remove.

## Variant B: the thesis is written afterwards

The order takes Kessler Semi to 3%, within both limits. The run is out of band and reaches
the change two days later. By then the stock is up 12%, and analysts credit the rise to a
rumoured data-centre contract.

### Expected run

1. **Lift.** The change touches `{orders}` and `{portfolio}`.
2. **Find candidates.** Thesis-first trade and rebalance, as upstream candidates.
3. **Distill.** The order carries a ticker, a size, and a time. The walk back along
   thesis-first trade reaches `{theses}` and then `{market data}`, and
   [distillation is bounded in time](/cyber-truss/model/canonical-execution/#distillation-reads-backward):
   it reads the data as it stood when the order went in. At that minute the data holds
   Orrin's recall announcement. The rise and the analysts' account of it come two days
   later, so they are outside the bound. Intent: *hold Kessler Semi for the market share it
   gains from Orrin's recall.*
4. **Criteria.** A thesis states the market-share mechanism and an invalidation condition:
   Orrin ships a replacement chip within one quarter. The position is within both limits.
5. **Select.** Thesis-first trade is strained, because `{theses}` lacks the thesis. The
   mandate already holds the limits, so rebalance is not strained. The arriving order meets
   the criteria, so the output carries no strain.
6. **Replay.** Thesis-first trade passes through `{market data}`, writes the recall thesis,
   and derives a 3% position.
7. **Compare.** Match. The replay derives the position the order already reached, so it
   emits no order.
8. **Leash.** The thesis is an owned write and proceeds. No order is emitted, so nothing
   waits for approval.

### Settled state

- `{theses}` holds the recall thesis, with the condition that would prove it wrong.
- `{orders}` and `{portfolio}` are unchanged.

Without the bound, the distiller would read the data as it is two days on, write a
data-centre thesis the trader never had, and the comparison would still report a match.
That is the backfill the [problem statement](/cyber-truss/model/#the-problem) describes: a
specification that fits the implementation because it was written from the implementation's
outcome. [Deriving criteria before the replay](/cyber-truss/model/canonical-execution/#criteria-are-derived-before-the-replay-not-after)
does not prevent it, because the leak is in distillation, before the criteria exist.

### Status: Unresolved

The run settles in the state the desk wanted, and it depends on two things the model has
not closed.

- The bound's form is open. Market data makes it mechanical here, since every price and
  every note has a timestamp. Declaring `{market data}` is also what puts the recall
  headline on the walk at all. The order alone does not carry it.
- The bound covers artifacts, not people. A distiller that asks the trader two days later
  gets an answer shaped by the rise, because no bound restricts what a person remembers.
  Deferring the ceremony, which is [half the guarantee](/cyber-truss/model/#the-guarantee),
  widens that window, and a position keeps collecting results while it waits.

## Variant C: the thesis is invalidated by the market

Take the portfolio settled by variant A. In week ten Orrin ships a replacement chip, the
condition the Kessler Semi thesis names. The feed job records the announcement in
`{market data}`. Nobody touches the mandate, the theses, the orders, or the portfolio.

### Expected run

1. **Lift.** The change touches `{market data}`.
2. **Find candidates.** No workflow owns or outputs `{market data}`. Its controller is the
   feed job, so there are no upstream candidates. Thesis-first trade reads `{market data}`
   as an input, so it is a
   [downstream candidate](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#a-change-finds-its-candidates).
3. **Distill.** There is nothing to walk back along. The intent is the change's own, and it
   is thin: *market data is current as of week ten.* A press release carries no intent of
   the portfolio's owner, and the run does not need one.
4. **Criteria.** The intent adds none. The criteria the specifications already state take
   part in the [join](/cyber-truss/model/join/#within-a-run), and the thesis states its
   own invalidation condition.
5. **Select.** Thesis-first trade is strained. `{theses}`, which it owns, no longer meets
   what its changed input states: the data now meets the Kessler Semi thesis's invalidation
   condition. Its input is not strained, since the data is fresh, so it does not wait.
6. **Replay.** The change landed in thesis-first trade's input, so the workflow
   [reads it](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#what-a-workflow-reads), diff included. It cannot
   write `{market data}`. At `{theses}` it closes the Kessler Semi thesis as invalidated. At
   `{portfolio}` it derives no position, and it emits a sell order to reach it.
7. **Compare.** The arriving change was data from the feed job, and the replay did not touch
   the data. Nothing disagrees.
8. **Leash.** Closing the thesis is an owned write and proceeds. The sell order is an output
   and goes to the trader for approval.
9. **Propagate.** Once the order fills, semiconductors fall further under 25%, so
   `{mandate}` to `{portfolio}` holds. `{theses}` to `{portfolio}` holds, because no
   position outlives its thesis.

### Settled state

- `{theses}` records the Kessler Semi thesis as invalidated in week ten.
- `{orders}` holds the approved sell.
- `{portfolio}` no longer holds Kessler Semi.
- `{mandate}` and `{market data}` are unchanged by the run.

### Status: Holds

The loop [starts only from a change](/cyber-truss/model/canonical-execution/#the-loop-starts-only-from-a-change),
and declaring `{market data}` gives the announcement one. The trading workflow is found by a
lookup over its declared input, not by noticing a stale thesis first. The earlier objection,
that a press release carries no intent of the portfolio's owner, is true and does not
matter. The intent the run needs is the thesis's, and the thesis states it as a criterion.

Two things remain outside the settled state.

- How fast the sale happens. Between the announcement and the fill, `{theses}` to
  `{portfolio}` is strained: a position is held past its invalidation. The sell order waits
  for approval, and nothing in this instance limits how long. That is the workflow's strain
  policy to state, as in variant A.
- A feed that stops. The freshness criterion on `{market data}` to `{theses}` goes false
  when the job fails, and the strain says so. A job that fails on every run starts a run
  on every tick, and what bounds that sequence is
  [open](/cyber-truss/model/open-questions/#what-bounds-retries-across-runs).

## What it tests

- [Outputs](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#three-roles) that cannot be revised. An order is
  emitted, so strain on it clears only by a superseding order, and variant A's breach window
  is the lifetime of that obligation.
- The [leash](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#leash) on an irreversible write. Every order the
  runs produce waits for the trader, while theses proceed on the criteria alone.
- [Criteria over the outcome](/cyber-truss/model/specification/#a-specification-is-intent-plus-criteria)
  where the earlier state cannot be restored. Variant A settles by a new order rather than
  by undoing the old one, and the criteria do not tell the two apart.
- The window between a change and its settled state, when the change acts on the world at
  once. The roles explain it. How long it may last is the strain policy's to state, and this
  instance states nothing.
- [The time bound on distillation](/cyber-truss/model/canonical-execution/#distillation-reads-backward)
  against hindsight. Variant B shows that deriving criteria before the replay protects the
  comparison and leaves distillation exposed, and that the bound closes the leak from data
  but not the leak from people.
- A change with almost no expression. An order is a ticker, a size, and a time.
  Distillation finds the intent only by walking back to `{market data}`, and the walk is
  there because the trade workflow declares the data as an input.
- [Downstream candidates](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#a-change-finds-its-candidates).
  Variant C reaches the trade workflow by a lookup over its input, from a feed job's write
  that carries no owner intent.
- [Declared roles](/cyber-truss/model/workflow/#direction-lives-in-the-workflow) as a guard.
  Thesis-first trade reads `{market data}` and owns the thesis, so no replay can rewrite the
  data to keep a thesis alive.
- A source brought inside the boundary. The market is outside the system until
  `{market data}` is declared, and its freshness is a
  [criterion over time](/cyber-truss/model/canonical-execution/#the-loop-starts-only-from-a-change).
- An artifact outside the repository. The broker holds `{orders}` and `{portfolio}`, and
  nothing in the model needs git to reason about them.
  [Lifting](/cyber-truss/model/artifact-sets/#lifting) does: a trade log has to be raised
  into artifact-set vocabulary the same way a diff is.
